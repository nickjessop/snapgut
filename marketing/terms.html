Terms

The deal, in plain language: what SnapGut does, what it does not claim, your responsibilities as operator, and what we each agree to.

What SnapGut is

SnapGut is a food and symptom diary that looks for patterns in your own log. It compares how often symptoms follow meals containing a given food against your own baseline of meals without it, and shows you the result with its confidence attached.

That is an association, not a cause. SnapGut is not a medical device, it does not diagnose anything, and it is not an allergy or intolerance test. Nothing it shows you is medical advice. If your symptoms are severe, persistent, or worrying, talk to a clinician — and take your export with you, that is part of what it is for.

Our food tagging is coarse keyword matching, not portion-aware laboratory data, so treat a trigger group as a hint worth testing rather than a verdict.

Self-hosted operation

You run SnapGut on your own hardware or cloud infrastructure. You are the operator. You are responsible for keeping the software updated, maintaining backups of the data directory, and controlling who can reach the instance over the network.

We provide the software as-is under its license. We do not operate your instance, we have no access to your data, and we accept no liability for data loss, downtime, or misconfiguration on your infrastructure.

Your data

Everything you log stays on the machine you run SnapGut on. No telemetry is collected, no data leaves your instance except where you configure an external AI provider, and no third-party service is contacted without your explicit configuration.

You own what you log. The software claims no rights over your entries, your notes, or your photos. You can export everything at any time and delete your data whenever you choose — both are controls in the app.

AI features

Meal recognition and insight text are generated by the AI provider you configure. When you use these features, the data you submit (a photo or a prompt) is sent to that provider under their terms. We do not operate the AI service and have no control over how it processes your data.

Models get things wrong. Check the ingredients listed before you rely on them, especially if you avoid a food for medical reasons, and edit them when they are off — your corrections are what make the pattern analysis worth reading.

Fair use

Use the app as a person using an app. The rate limits exist to protect your instance from accidental abuse, not to gatekeep. Do not remove them unless you understand the consequences for the AI provider you are calling.

Availability and liability

SnapGut is provided as-is, without a guarantee that it will be available at any particular moment or that it will suit a particular purpose. We may change, add, or remove features as the project develops. As an operator you accept the risk that an upgrade may require manual intervention.

Logging works offline because it is stored on your device. AI recognition, AI insights, and sync need a network connection and depend on services you configure, so they can be unavailable even when the app is not.

Keep your own backups of the data directory. Browser storage can be evicted when a device runs short of space, which is why export and backup are always available.
